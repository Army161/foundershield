FounderShield is a reverse proxy that sits in front of your site. It verifies
that visitors are real browsers, closes the routes that should be closed, and
keeps a tamper-evident record of everything it refused.

The blocking is the part everyone expects. The record is the part that matters.

## Why the record is the product

The hard part of acting on a scraping incident is not stopping it — it is
proving what happened. By the time you know you need evidence, the requests are
already gone. FounderShield collects that evidence continuously, in a form your
own account cannot alter, so it exists before you need it.

<Note>
FounderShield produces a record. It does not produce a legal conclusion. Take
the export to a lawyer — do not take a dashboard for one.
</Note>

## What it is not

- Not a guarantee that scraping stops. Determined scrapers with real browsers
  get through; what changes is that you have a record of them.
- Not a claim that bypassing it is a crime. That depends on your content, your
  jurisdiction, and facts a proxy cannot see.
- Not a law firm. The generated terms, NDAs and notices are templates.

## Architecture

```
visitor ──▶ Cloudflare Worker ──▶ your origin
               │
               ├─ access lists      IP / user-agent allow and block
               ├─ automation score  header analysis, no JS required
               ├─ rate limit        sharded Durable Object counters
               ├─ auth gate         protected routes
               ├─ terms acceptance  clickwrap, versioned and signed
               └─ browser check     proof-of-work + rolling-key token
                        │
                        └─▶ evidence log ──▶ Supabase (append-only)
```

Three pieces: a Cloudflare Worker at the edge, a Supabase database holding
projects and evidence, and a Next.js dashboard. Customers install nothing —
they point two DNS records at the proxy.
