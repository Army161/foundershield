Evidence is the part of FounderShield worth paying for. Everything else is
commodity bot mitigation.

## How records are protected

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="Hashed on write">
A database trigger hashes every row on insert. The hash covers the observation
columns — address, time, what was requested, which layer stopped it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Sealed in ranges">
A seal covers a contiguous range of events and chains to its predecessor. Seals
cannot be created twice over the same range with no new events in between.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Verified by recomputation">
Verification recomputes each row's hash from its *current* contents rather than
trusting the stored value. An attacker who edits a row and recomputes its stored
hash still breaks the chained digest — and verification reports which rows were
altered.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Beyond your own reach">
Your account has no permission to update or delete evidence. That restriction is
what makes the record worth anything: a log its beneficiary can edit proves
nothing.
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What the dashboard distinguishes

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Allowed | Served normally |
| Challenged | Asked to prove it was a browser |
| Blocked | Refused |
| Enforced | Whether the decision was actually acted on, or merely recorded |

## Burden analysis

Per-request cost is recorded, then aggregated per actor: requests, bytes, origin
time consumed, active span, and peak per-minute rate. These are the only numbers
a trespass-to-chattels argument can rest on — and if they turn out unremarkable,
it is better to know that before relying on the claim.

<Note>
Seals prove internal consistency. They do not prove the data was true when
written, and there is no external timestamping yet. See [Limitations](/reference/limitations).
</Note>
