These are disclosed up front rather than discovered later.

## Technical

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Search crawler verification is inherited | Cloudflare's verified-bot signal is trusted rather than an independent reverse-DNS check being performed |
| Evidence seals are self-generated | They prove internal consistency, not that the data was true when written. No external timestamping yet |
| Request counts are estimates | Ordinary traffic is sampled |
| Retention is not auto-enforced | Per-plan retention windows are not yet enforced automatically |

## Legal

FounderShield draws a deliberate line between four different things, and does
not let them blur:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
<Card title="Technical observation" icon="eye">
A request arrived with these characteristics.
</Card>
<Card title="Evidence" icon="folder">
A tamper-evident record that the observation was made.
</Card>
<Card title="Interpretation" icon="magnifying-glass">
What the pattern of observations suggests.
</Card>
<Card title="Legal conclusion" icon="scale-balanced">
Not ours to draw. Take it to a lawyer.
</Card>
</CardGroup>

Specifically, FounderShield does **not** claim that:

- Scraping protected content is automatically illegal
- Bypassing it automatically constitutes a federal crime
- Evidence is immutable — it is tamper-**evident**, which is a different claim
- Any litigation will succeed
- Generated notices are legal advice
- Signing provides a qualified electronic signature

Anti-circumvention law as applied to bot mitigation is genuinely contested, and
scraping a page you published openly is often not unauthorised access at all.
What FounderShield provides is the record you would otherwise not have.
